Numerical ODE, DAE & Co-Simulation Solvers ​

Implementation: packages/simulate/src/solvers/ and packages/runtime/src/solvers/

1. Tsitouras 5(4) Adaptive Runge-Kutta (Tsit5) ​

Academic Citations ​

  • Tsitouras, Ch. (2011). "Runge-Kutta pairs of order 5 (4) satisfying only the first column simplifying assumption."
    Computers & Mathematics with Applications, 62(2), pp. 770–775.
    DOI: 10.1016/j.camwa.2011.06.002
  • Rackauckas, C., & Nie, Q. (2017). "DifferentialEquations.jl – A Performant and Feature-Rich Ecosystem for Solving Differential Equations in Julia."
    Journal of Open Research Software, 5(1), 15. DOI: 10.5334/jors.151

ModelScript Rationale & Modifications ​

For non-stiff systems (e.g. mechanical multi-body dynamics, kinematics, flight control), high-order adaptive explicit Runge-Kutta pairs offer peak throughput without Newton-Raphson linear algebra costs. Tsit5 demonstrates significantly lower error coefficients and wider stability regions than classical Dormand-Prince (DOPRI5).

  • Modifications: Employs the First-Same-As-Last (FSAL) optimization eliminating stage 1 evaluation in successive steps; embeds a 5th-order continuous dense output polynomial for sub-interval event zero-crossing localization.

2. TR-BDF2 Composite Stiff Solver ​

Academic Citations ​

  • Bank, R. E., Coughran, W. M., et al. (1985). "Transient simulation of silicon devices and circuits."
    IEEE Transactions on CAD of Integrated Circuits, 4(4), pp. 436–446.
    DOI: 10.1109/TCAD.1985.1270142
  • Hosea, M. E., & Shampine, L. F. (1996). "Analysis and implementation of TR-BDF2."
    Applied Numerical Mathematics, 20(1-2), pp. 21–37.
    DOI: 10.1016/0168-9274(95)00115-8

ModelScript Rationale & Modifications ​

Cyber-physical models featuring frequent hybrid discrete events (switches, impacts, hysteresis) severely penalize multistep BDF solvers (which must drop to order 1 after every event). TR-BDF2 is an L-stable one-step composite method pairing a trapezoidal half-step with a BDF2 completion step:

  • Modifications: Shares the diagonal Jacobian coefficient d=1−1/2 across both internal stages, requiring only one sparse LU factorization per time step; connects directly with ModelScript's in-WASM sparse matrix kernel (sparse_lu.ts).

3. RODAS-4P Linearly Implicit Rosenbrock Solver ​

Academic Citations ​

  • Steinebach, G. (1995). "Order-reduction of ROW-methods for DAEs and method of lines applications."
    TH Darmstadt Preprint 1742.
  • Hairer, E., & Wanner, G. (1996). Solving Ordinary Differential Equations II: Stiff and Differential-Algebraic Problems.
    Springer-Verlag. Section IV.8.

ModelScript Rationale & Modifications ​

Solves stiff index-1 DAEs and method-of-lines continuum discretizations without iterative non-linear Newton solves. It solves only a sequence of linear systems sharing the same system matrix I−γhJ.

  • Modifications: Optimized for in-WASM sparse Jacobian caching; utilizes automatic differentiation coloring (coloring.ts) for fast Jacobian updates.

4. Itô Stochastic Differential Equations (SDE) Solver ​

Academic Citations ​

  • Rößler, A. (2010). "Runge-Kutta methods for Itô stochastic differential equations with additive noise."
    SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis, 48(3), pp. 922–952. DOI: 10.1137/09076636X
  • Kloeden, P. E., & Platen, E. (1992). Numerical Solution of Stochastic Differential Equations.
    Springer. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-662-12616-5

ModelScript Rationale & Modifications ​

Physical systems operate under thermal noise, turbulence, and sensor fluctuations:

dx=f(t,x)dt+g(t,x)dWt
  • Modifications: Employs an in-WASM Xoshiro256pp PRNG with Box-Muller transformation; provides both fixed-step Euler-Maruyama (strong order 0.5) and adaptive Rößler SRIW1 (strong order 1.5) integrators for Monte Carlo uncertainty rollouts.

5. Co-Simulation Master Orchestrator ​

Academic Citations ​

  • Bastian, J., et al. (2011). "Master for Co-Simulation Using FMI."
    Proceedings of the 8th International Modelica Conference, pp. 115–120. DOI: 10.3384/ecp11063115
  • Kübler, R., & Schiehlen, W. (2000). "Two methods of simulator coupling."
    Mathematical and Computer Modelling of Dynamical Systems, 6(2), pp. 93–113. DOI: 10.1076/1387-3954(200006)6:2;1-M;FT093

ModelScript Rationale & Modifications ​

Coordinates multi-rate, multi-vendor FMI Functional Mock-up Units (FMUs) and SSP topologies. Implements Richardson extrapolation error estimation to dynamically adapt the inter-simulator communication step size Hcomm, preventing divergence while maximizing simulation speed.

6. Dommel Companion-Circuit EMT & Bergeron Traveling-Wave Solver ​

Academic Citations ​

  • Dommel, H. W. (1969). "Digital Computer Solution of Electromagnetic Transients in Single- and Multiphase Networks."
    IEEE Transactions on Power Apparatus and Systems, PAS-88(4), pp. 388–399.
    DOI: 10.1109/TPAS.1969.292459
  • Marti, J. R. (1982). "Accurate Modelling of Frequency-Dependent Transmission Lines in Electromagnetic Transient Simulations."
    IEEE Transactions on Power Apparatus and Systems, PAS-101(1), pp. 147–157.
    DOI: 10.1109/TPAS.1982.317332

ModelScript Rationale & Modifications ​

Implemented in EmtSolver (packages/simulate/src/solvers/emt/emt-solver.ts), this solver targets microsecond-scale electromagnetic transients, power electronics switching (IGBTs/breakers), and distributed transmission lines imported from @modelscript/pscad:

  • Trapezoidal Companion Models & Constant Nodal Matrix: Inductors (GL=Δt2L) and capacitors (GC=2CΔt) are discretized into Norton companion equivalents Gv(t)=ihist(t−Δt)+isrc(t), allowing G to remain constant between switching events.
  • Critical Damping Adjustment (CDA): Automatically transitions from Trapezoidal to Backward Euler integration (GL=ΔtL,GC=CΔt) for two half-steps (Δt/2) immediately after any breaker or semiconductor switching discontinuity to suppress artificial dv/dt numerical oscillations.
  • Bergeron Traveling-Wave Line Decoupling & Norton FMU Interface: Distributed transmission lines with propagation delay τ=ℓ/v≥Δt decouple the nodal admittance matrix into independent sub-grids using interpolated history ring buffers, and expose getNortonEquivalent(portNode) (Ieq,Geq) for zero-delay FMI Co-Simulation coupling.

7. High-Order Taylor Model Flowpipe Integration ​

Academic Citations ​

  • Makino, K., & Berz, M. (2003). "Taylor Models and Other Validated Functional Inclusion Methods."
    International Journal of Pure and Applied Mathematics, 4(4), pp. 379–456.
  • Chen, X., Ábrahám, E., & Sankaranarayanan, S. (2013). "Flow*: An Analyzer for Non-Linear Hybrid Systems."
    Computer Aided Verification (CAV 2013), LNCS vol. 8044, pp. 258–263.
    DOI: 10.1007/978-3-642-39799-8_18

ModelScript Rationale & Modifications ​

Implemented in TaylorModel (packages/runtime/src/analysis/wasm_taylor_model.ts), validated Taylor Model arithmetic bounds all trajectories of a non-linear ODE/DAE system subject to uncertain initial sets x(0)∈X0 and parameter intervals without wrapping-effect blowup:

  • Polynomial + Interval Remainder Representation: Represents each state reachable tube over normalized domain z∈[−1,1]d as a multivariate polynomial Pk(z) of order k plus a sound interval remainder I=[r―,r―], where truncated higher-order terms (>k) are conservatively swept into I.
  • Picard-Lindelöf Validated Operator: Evaluates the contractive Picard integral operator P(X)(t)=X0+∫0tf(s,X(s))ds directly on Taylor models to construct guaranteed continuous-time flowpipes consumed by ContinuousSafetyOracle and SysML v2 hybrid state-machine reachability (hybrid-flowpipe-bridge.ts).

Upstream & Downstream Pipeline Connections ​

  • Upstream Inputs:
    • DAE blocks partitioned and torn by BltEngine and TornBlock.
    • Sparse Jacobians computed via Dual Numbers & Graph Coloring.
  • Downstream Consumers:
    • Powers msx simulate, msx optimize, and the Web IDE charting canvas.
    • Feeds state trajectories and validated flowpipes into Signal Temporal Logic (STL) Monitor & Theory Coordinator and formal verification oracles.
